# Brikensa | Building a B2B Commercial Engine From Zero

Building a regional B2B sales operation from the ground up, growing it to 30+ representatives, and creating the coverage, capability, and productivity required to sell a 300+ product portfolio profitably.

**Company:** Brikensa España S.A.  
**Role:** Regional Sales Director  
**Career Span:** February 2006 – November 2009  
**Geography:** Northern Spain  
**Business:** B2B chemical products and distribution  
**Mandate:** Build and lead the regional commercial operation from zero  
**Focus:** B2B Sales | Team Building | Territory Expansion | Sales Productivity | Distribution | Profitable Growth

---

## 01. Executive Snapshot

I moved from the United States to Spain to build Brikensa's commercial operation across Northern Spain.

There was no regional sales organization for me to inherit at the scale required.

I had to build it.

The company sold more than 300 products into the B2B market.

My responsibility was to build the organization required to take that portfolio across the region.

That meant:

- Recruiting the sales team from zero
- Developing commercial representatives
- Expanding regional coverage
- Establishing sales discipline
- Building product knowledge
- Managing individual and team performance
- Increasing sales productivity
- Growing revenue while protecting margins

Over time, I built and led a commercial organization of more than 30 representatives.

The results included:

**Approximately 78% revenue growth in the first year.**

**Approximately €10K to €12K in monthly revenue per representative.**

**Approximately 40% operating margins.**

The numbers mattered.

But the core of the mandate was building what produced them.

A 30+ person field organization had to be recruited and developed.

Regional coverage had to be created.

A large product portfolio had to become manageable for the sales team.

Performance needed to be measured.

Representatives needed to become productive.

And growth needed to make economic sense.

The operating logic was:

**Build the Team**
↓
**Create Coverage**
↓
**Develop Capability**
↓
**Drive Productivity**
↓
**Measure**
↓
**Improve**
↓
**Scale**

This became one of my earliest lessons in building commercial organizations.

> **Before you can scale sales, you need an organization capable of producing them.**

## 02. The Starting Point

The mandate was to build the regional commercial operation across Northern Spain.

The business operated in B2B chemical products and distribution, with a portfolio of more than 300 products.

That created two immediate requirements.

First, we needed commercial coverage.

Second, the sales team needed enough product knowledge to turn a broad portfolio into relevant customer conversations.

The challenge had six parts:

**People**
↓
Build the sales organization

**Coverage**
↓
Reach the regional market

**Capability**
↓
Develop product and commercial knowledge

**Execution**
↓
Create consistent sales activity

**Productivity**
↓
Generate sufficient revenue per representative

**Economics**
↓
Grow while maintaining strong margins

These elements had to work together.

Adding representatives without sufficient productivity would add cost.

Expanding coverage without disciplined execution would create activity without enough return.

A 300+ product portfolio without sufficient product knowledge would create complexity rather than opportunity.

The objective was not simply to hire more salespeople.

It was to build a productive commercial organization.

---

## 03. Build the Sales Organization

The first requirement was people.

I built the regional sales organization from zero and grew it to more than 30 representatives across Northern Spain.

Recruitment was only the beginning.

Each representative needed to become productive.

That required clear expectations, product knowledge, commercial discipline, and performance management.

The process was:

**Recruit**
↓
**Develop**
↓
**Deploy**
↓
**Manage**
↓
**Measure**
↓
**Improve**

As the team grew, my role changed.

Initially, the priority was building capacity.

Once the organization reached greater scale, the priority became managing that capacity effectively.

With more than 30 representatives in the field, performance could not depend on my involvement in every sale.

The organization needed structure.

Representatives needed to know what was expected.

Performance needed to be visible.

Underperformance needed to be identified.

Strong practices needed to be reinforced.

The team needed enough commercial and product knowledge to operate effectively in each territory.

This established an important distinction:

**Headcount is not capacity.**

People become commercial capacity when they have the knowledge, expectations, structure, and management required to perform.

The objective was not to build the largest possible sales force.

It was to build a productive one.

---

## 04. Build Regional Coverage

The next requirement was coverage.

Northern Spain represented a large commercial territory.

We needed enough presence across the region to identify opportunities, acquire customers, develop accounts, and generate repeat business.

But geographic presence alone was not enough.

Each territory needed enough commercial potential to justify the capacity deployed against it.

The logic was:

**Territory**
↓
**Commercial Capacity**
↓
**Customer Coverage**
↓
**Account Development**
↓
**Repeat Business**
↓
**Territory Performance**

As the team expanded, regional coverage increased.

The management question was not simply:

**How many representatives do we have?**

It was:

**What is each part of the organization producing?**

That kept the focus on productive coverage rather than headcount.

---

## 05. Turn Product Breadth Into Sales Capability

The portfolio included more than 300 products.

That breadth created commercial opportunity, but it also created complexity.

Representatives could not approach every customer with every product.

They needed to understand the customer's requirement and identify which products were relevant.

The commercial sequence was:

**Customer Need**
↓
**Relevant Product**
↓
**Commercial Conversation**
↓
**Sale**
↓
**Repeat Opportunity**

The objective was not to memorize hundreds of products.

It was to develop enough product knowledge to navigate the portfolio effectively.

That also created opportunities within existing accounts.

A customer relationship did not necessarily represent one product sale.

A strong understanding of the portfolio could uncover additional needs and expand the relationship.

The portfolio became more valuable as the team's ability to use it improved.

> **Product breadth creates value when the sales team can connect it to a real customer need.**

---

## 06. Build Sales Productivity

As the organization grew, productivity became a critical measure of performance.

More representatives created greater commercial capacity.

They also created greater cost.

The team therefore needed to produce enough revenue to justify the resources deployed.

We tracked performance at the individual representative level.

Over time, representatives generated approximately:

**€10K to €12K in monthly revenue per representative.**

This provided a practical measure of whether additional commercial capacity was translating into additional revenue.

The question was no longer only:

**How much are we selling?**

It was also:

**How productive is the organization producing those sales?**

That distinction matters when scaling.

Revenue can increase because headcount increases.

If productivity falls at the same time, the commercial model may be getting weaker.

The stronger sequence is:

**Add Capacity**
↓
**Develop Capability**
↓
**Drive Productivity**
↓
**Generate Revenue**
↓
**Protect Margin**
↓
**Scale**

Every additional representative needed to become productive.

Every territory needed to justify the capacity deployed against it.

Growth needed to strengthen the business, not simply make the sales organization larger.

> **Commercial scale is measured by what the organization produces as it grows.**

---

## 07. Protect the Economics

Growth alone was not enough.

As the sales organization expanded, the economics needed to remain strong.

More representatives increased capacity.

They also increased cost.

The business therefore needed to keep four variables connected:

**Coverage**
↓
**Productivity**
↓
**Revenue**
↓
**Margin**

The regional business maintained operating margins of approximately:

**40%.**

This mattered because it showed that expansion and productivity were translating into strong economics.

Three questions therefore stayed connected:

**Are we growing?**

**Are our representatives productive?**

**Are we protecting margin?**

A larger sales organization was useful only if it produced a stronger business.

> **Growth creates value when the economics remain strong as the organization expands.**

## 10. What I Carried Forward

Several principles from this experience continued to shape how I approached larger commercial and general management roles.

### Build Capacity Before Expecting Scale

Growth requires enough people to reach the market.

But additional headcount only creates value when those people become productive.

### Measure Productivity, Not Only Revenue

Total revenue can hide weaknesses.

Productivity shows whether additional capacity is producing additional value.

### Build Product Capability

A broad portfolio creates opportunity only when the sales organization knows how to connect products with customer needs.

### Expand Coverage With a Business Case

Geographic expansion should follow commercial opportunity.

Deploy capacity where the market can support it.

### Keep Growth and Margin Connected

Revenue is one measure.

Productivity, cost, and margin determine whether that growth is strengthening the business.

### Manage the Drivers

Revenue appears at the end of the system.

Before it come:

**People**
↓
**Capability**
↓
**Coverage**
↓
**Activity**
↓
**Productivity**
↓
**Revenue**
↓
**Margin**

Understanding those drivers makes performance easier to manage.
