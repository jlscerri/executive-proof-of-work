# From Market Turnarounds to Global Scale

12 years of increasing responsibility across Europe, LATAM, and global markets, progressing from commercial leadership to country management, business turnarounds, and ultimately a global mandate across more than 15 international markets.

**Company:** EF Education First  
**Career Span:** 2009–2021  
**Geography:** Europe | LATAM | Global  
**Progression:** Sales Manager → Regional Sales Manager LATAM → Country Manager Peru → Country Manager Chile → VP Sales & Marketing Global  
**Mandates:** Commercial Growth | Multi-Market Leadership | Country Management | Turnaround | P&L | Organizational Leadership | International Expansion | Global Operating Model

---

## 01. Executive Snapshot

I spent more than 11 years at EF Education First.

I entered as a Sales Manager in Europe.

I left as a Global VP based at the company's headquarters in Zurich.

Between those roles, I moved through five progressively larger leadership mandates:

**Sales Manager, Europe**  
↓  
**Regional Sales Manager, LATAM**  
↓  
**Country Manager, Peru**  
↓  
**Country Manager, Chile**  
↓  
**VP Sales & Marketing, Global**

Each move increased the scope of responsibility.

But more importantly, each introduced a different business problem.

In Spain, the mandate was to strengthen commercial performance.

In LATAM, I was asked to bring the commercial practices developed in Spain into multiple markets and adapt them to different local realities.

In Peru, I inherited a business that had experienced six consecutive years of stagnant performance and operating losses.

In Chile, I took responsibility for a larger country operation and a cross-functional organization of more than 100 people.

At Global HQ, I helped build and scale a new business across more than 15 international markets.

The progression was consistent:

**Deliver**
↓
**Take on Greater Responsibility**
↓
**Enter a More Complex Environment**
↓
**Diagnose**
↓
**Build**
↓
**Deliver Again**

Selected results across the progression included:

- 34% sales growth in Spain
- Spain becoming EF's #1 global market for profitability and overall business performance
- 40% regional growth across Mexico, Colombia, Peru, and Venezuela
- 30% improvement in SQL conversion across LATAM
- 40% revenue growth in Peru
- First profitable year in EF Peru's history after six years of stagnation and losses
- 30% revenue growth in Chile
- Two new regional sales hubs generating 12% incremental revenue
- 25% improvement in SQL quality in Chile
- 18% reduction in CPL
- 17% EBITDA growth in Chile
- Leadership of a cross-functional organization of more than 100 people
- Launch and scale of a new global business across more than 15 international markets
- 35% year-over-year recurring revenue growth
- Leadership of a 90+ person matrix organization

The most important development across those years was how my role changed.

Early on, I could directly influence much of the result.

As responsibility increased, performance depended increasingly on other leaders, stronger organizations, clear ownership, and common operating practices.

My role evolved from managing performance directly to building organizations capable of delivering performance through others.

> **As the mandate became larger, my job became less about personally driving the result and more about building the organization capable of delivering it.**

## 03. Spain: Build Commercial Discipline

My EF career started in Spain.

The mandate was to improve sales performance, strengthen execution, expand market coverage, and build a commercial organization capable of delivering consistently.

The focus was practical:

- Pipeline
- Forecasting
- Sales activity
- Conversion
- Market coverage
- Team performance
- Management visibility
- Accountability

The objective was not simply to ask the sales organization for more.

We needed to understand what produced the result.

Where was pipeline coming from?

Where were opportunities being lost?

Which teams were performing?

Where did managers need to intervene?

How reliable was the forecast?

Were commercial activities translating into outcomes?

The management logic was:

**Target**
↓
**Pipeline**
↓
**Activity**
↓
**Conversion**
↓
**Forecast**
↓
**Result**
↓
**Review**
↓
**Action**

We strengthened pipeline management, forecasting, sales campaigns, regional team leadership, and performance management.

We also expanded the commercial footprint through regional sales offices.

The results were significant.

**Sales increased approximately 34%.**

Spain became **EF's #1 global market for profitability and overall business performance**.

The operation became an internal benchmark for commercial execution.

That performance led directly to my next mandate.

I was asked to move into LATAM and bring the commercial know-how and management practices developed in Spain into a region with very different market conditions.

That introduced a new question:

**How do you transfer what works without assuming every market works the same way?**

The answer became important throughout the rest of my career.

> **Transfer the principle. Adapt the execution.**

## 04. LATAM: Transfer the Know-How, Adapt the Execution

The move to LATAM changed the nature of my role.

I was now working across Mexico, Colombia, Peru, and Venezuela, partnering with country leadership teams to improve commercial performance across markets with different customers, teams, competitive environments, and levels of maturity.

The mandate came from the success we had built in Spain.

But bringing that know-how into LATAM could not mean copying Spain.

I needed to separate what could travel from what needed to remain local.

### What Could Transfer

- Pipeline management
- Sales process
- Qualification
- Forecasting
- Performance management
- Commercial KPIs
- Team coaching
- Accountability

### What Needed to Adapt

- Customer behavior
- Demand generation
- Messaging
- Market maturity
- Partnerships
- Competition
- Team capability
- Commercial priorities

The model became:

**Common Commercial Discipline**
↓
**Local Market Diagnosis**
↓
**Adapted Execution**
↓
**Measure**
↓
**Learn**
↓
**Improve**

This was also my first significant experience improving performance through other market leaders rather than controlling execution directly.

I needed to understand why markets performed differently.

Identify practices that could travel.

Coach local leaders.

And avoid imposing solutions that did not fit the market.

The results included:

**Approximately 40% regional growth across Mexico, Colombia, Peru, and Venezuela.**

**Approximately 30% improvement in SQL conversion.**

The larger lesson was clear.

Scale did not require every market to operate identically.

It required common commercial discipline combined with informed local execution.

## 05. Peru: From Commercial Leadership to Business Turnaround

Peru was my first full Country Manager mandate.

The business had experienced six consecutive years of stagnant performance and operating losses.

The challenge was no longer limited to sales.

I was responsible for making the business work.

That required looking across:

- Commercial performance
- Marketing
- Customer acquisition
- Partnerships
- Organization
- Leadership
- Business planning
- Costs
- Profitability

The first task was diagnosis.

More commercial pressure would not solve a structural performance problem.

We needed to understand why the business had remained stuck and change the conditions producing that result.

### Rebuild the Commercial Engine

We strengthened the connection between marketing and sales.

Global campaigns were adapted to the Peruvian market.

Demand generation became more relevant to local customers.

Funnel performance became a management priority.

Sales execution became more disciplined.

The results included:

**Landing page conversion approximately doubled.**

**Organic lead generation increased approximately 55%.**

### Build Local Distribution

Partnerships became another important part of the turnaround.

We expanded relationships with more than 20 schools and strategic influencers.

These relationships created additional market access, credibility, and customer acquisition opportunities.

**New customer acquisition increased approximately 30%.**

This reinforced an important principle.

Local growth often depends on relationships and channels that cannot be designed entirely from headquarters.

Country leadership needs to understand where customers are, who influences them, and how trust works locally.

### Rebuild the Organization

Commercial changes alone were not enough.

The organization also needed to change.

I restructured and led the cross-functional team across sales, marketing, and business planning.

Accountability became clearer.

Marketing and sales became more connected.

Management focused more directly on business outcomes.

The operating chain became:

**Marketing**
↓
**Demand**
↓
**Sales**
↓
**Customer Acquisition**
↓
**Revenue**
↓
**Costs**
↓
**Profitability**

As Country Manager, I could no longer optimize one function independently.

I had to manage how the functions worked together.

### Restore Growth and Profitability

The turnaround changed the business trajectory.

**Revenue increased approximately 40%.**

After six consecutive years of stagnation and operating losses:

**EF Peru delivered the first profitable year in its history.**

The result demonstrated that the market itself was not the fundamental constraint.

When the commercial model, local execution, organization, and management practices changed, the business result changed.

## 06. Chile: Scale a Larger Country Business

The Peru turnaround led to another increase in responsibility.

Chile presented a different challenge.

This was not another turnaround.

The business had greater scale, a stronger market position, and a cross-functional organization of more than 100 people.

The mandate was to accelerate growth while strengthening the organization required to sustain it.

The management question changed again:

**How do you take a functioning business and make it materially stronger without destabilizing what already works?**

The focus included:

- Revenue growth
- Commercial efficiency
- Marketing performance
- National coverage
- Leadership capability
- Organizational execution
- Customer acquisition
- KPI governance
- Financial performance

### Expand the National Footprint

One opportunity was geographic coverage.

We opened two regional sales hubs to increase market presence and bring commercial execution closer to customers.

The expansion generated approximately:

**12% incremental revenue.**

Each expansion required a clear business case.

Where was the demand?

Could the market support additional capacity?

What local capability was required?

How would performance be measured?

Would the additional footprint justify the investment?

Growth increasingly required disciplined decisions about where to deploy people, capital, and management attention.

### Improve Commercial Efficiency

We also improved the connection between marketing and sales.

The objective was better demand, not simply more leads.

The results included:

**SQL quality increased approximately 25%.**

**Cost per Lead decreased approximately 18%.**

The question was simple:

**Are we creating more commercial value from the resources we deploy?**

### Lead Through a Larger Organization

Leading more than 100 professionals required a different management model.

Direct intervention could no longer be the primary mechanism.

Managers needed clear responsibilities.

KPIs needed owners.

Priorities needed to be understood.

Performance needed to be visible.

Leaders needed enough authority to act.

My role increasingly became:

**Set Direction**
↓
**Build Leadership Capability**
↓
**Clarify Ownership**
↓
**Create Visibility**
↓
**Review Performance**
↓
**Intervene Where Necessary**
↓
**Let Leaders Lead**

The larger the organization became, the more performance depended on the quality of the leaders around me.

### Connect Growth With Profitability

The business delivered:

**Approximately 30% revenue growth.**

Chile became **EF's second-largest business in Latin America.**

At the same time:

**EBITDA grew approximately 17%.**

Revenue growth alone was not enough.

The business needed to convert growth into stronger financial performance.

The operating logic was:

**Demand**
↓
**Conversion**
↓
**Revenue**
↓
**Operational Capacity**
↓
**Cost Discipline**
↓
**EBITDA**

Chile reinforced an important leadership shift.

In Peru, I had learned to manage the whole business.

In Chile, I increasingly learned to manage the business through leaders.

## 07. Global VP: Build the System for International Scale

The next mandate changed the scale again.

I moved to EF's headquarters in Zurich as VP Sales & Marketing.

EF was building a new global business.

My mandate was to help launch and scale it across more than 15 international markets in Europe, Asia, and the Americas.

The challenge was no longer running one business.

It was building a model that multiple markets could execute.

Markets needed clear answers to:

- Who is the customer?
- How do we go to market?
- What should remain globally consistent?
- What needs to adapt locally?
- Who owns which decisions?
- How should markets and Global HQ work together?
- Which KPIs matter?
- How do we identify problems early?
- How do we scale without creating unnecessary complexity?

At this level, direct control was impossible.

Performance depended on clarity, leadership capability, market ownership, information, and operating discipline.

The leadership model had evolved:

**Direct Execution**
↓
**Country Leadership**
↓
**Leadership Through Managers**
↓
**Leadership Through an Operating Model**

### Build the Global Operating Model

The business needed a common foundation.

We built the operating model around:

- GTM
- Commercial framework
- Market execution
- Leadership structure
- Governance
- Performance management
- Operating cadence
- Partnerships
- Localization

The objective was not to make every market identical.

Global scale required a balance between common standards and local judgment.

The principle was:

**Create the framework globally. Enable execution locally.**

### Align More Than 15 Markets

Operating across more than 15 markets created a different management problem.

The organization needed alignment without requiring every decision to move through Global HQ.

That required clarity around:

What is the global priority?

What does success look like?

Which KPIs matter?

Who owns the market result?

What can markets decide independently?

Where is regional or global alignment required?

When should leadership intervene?

Too little structure creates fragmentation.

Too much central control slows execution.

The operating model needed to provide direction without removing local accountability.

### Build Leadership Capability

The organization grew to more than 90 professionals working through a matrix across international markets.

At that scale, the quality of leadership became critical.

We established clearer:

- Leadership responsibilities
- Market ownership
- Decision rights
- Performance expectations
- Management cadence
- Accountability
- Cross-market coordination

My role increasingly shifted from solving individual problems to ensuring the organization had the people, information, structure, and authority required to solve them.

The question became:

**Can the organization solve this without depending on me?**

### Localize Commercial Execution

A global operating model could provide structure.

It could not replace local market understanding.

Customer behavior differed.

Channels differed.

Competition differed.

Market maturity differed.

Partnership opportunities differed.

Brand strength differed.

The common model therefore required local execution.

Selected market results included:

**France grew approximately 23%.**

**Italy grew approximately 22%.**

**Spain grew approximately 15%.**

The lesson was consistent with what I had first learned in LATAM.

Define what should be common.

Give local leaders room to execute what must be local.

### Build Partnerships and Market Reach

We also expanded the international partner ecosystem through partnerships, sponsorships, events, and local market initiatives.

These supported:

- Market access
- Brand visibility
- Credibility
- Distribution
- Customer engagement
- Strategic relationships

One global partnership and campaign strategy generated more than:

**$100M in earned media value.**

The operating principle was straightforward.

A partnership needed to serve a business objective.

Activity alone was not enough.

### Create Visibility Across Markets

Global leadership could not operate through isolated updates and individual conversations.

We needed a common way to understand performance.

The management chain became:

**Business Objective**
↓
**Market Performance**
↓
**KPIs**
↓
**Ownership**
↓
**Review**
↓
**Decision**
↓
**Action**

The purpose was not more reporting.

It was better management.

Which markets were performing?

Which were not?

Why?

Was the issue demand?

Commercial execution?

Leadership?

Localization?

Resources?

Market conditions?

The system helped direct management attention toward the problems where intervention could create value.

### Scale the Business

The new business expanded across more than 15 international markets.

The organization grew to more than 90 professionals.

And the business delivered approximately:

**35% year-over-year recurring revenue growth.**

At country level, I could still influence a significant part of the operation personally.

At global level, that approach would have failed.

Performance now depended on:

**Leaders**
↓
**Clear Priorities**
↓
**Common Standards**
↓
**Local Judgment**
↓
**KPIs**
↓
**Operating Cadence**
↓
**Accountability**

That was the operating model behind international scale.

## 10. What Stayed Constant

The management model evolved.

Several principles remained consistent.

### Understand Before Acting

The visible problem is not always the real problem.

Understand what is producing the result before deciding what to change.

### Manage the Drivers

Revenue and profitability are outcomes.

Management starts with the commercial, operational, organizational, and customer drivers underneath them.

### Transfer Principles, Not Formulas

What works in one market can provide useful knowledge.

It does not eliminate the need to understand the next market.

### Make Ownership Clear

People need to know:

What do I own?

What can I decide?

How will success be measured?

When do I need to escalate?

### Build Capability Around You

As the organization becomes stronger, it should become less dependent on the executive.

### Connect Growth With Economics

Revenue, acquisition efficiency, organizational capacity, investment, costs, and profitability need to be managed together.

### Stay Close to Execution

Delegation does not mean distance.

An executive still needs enough visibility to understand what is happening, why it is happening, and where intervention is required.

### Learn From Execution

Plans create direction.

Execution creates information.

Use that information to improve the next decision.

## 13. The Operating Principle

If I had to reduce more than 11 years of progression to one leadership principle, it would be this:

> **As responsibility increases, leadership has to move from producing the result to building the organization that produces the result.**

A Country Manager cannot make every decision.

A regional leader cannot run every country.

A global executive cannot manage every market.

The job changes.

Set the direction.

Build strong leaders.

Make ownership clear.

Create visibility.

Put decisions at the right level.

Stay close enough to understand the business.

Intervene where your involvement creates value.

Then allow the organization to execute.

The objective is not to make the executive indispensable.

It is to build an organization capable of performing without depending on the executive.
