# Executive Proof of Work

### Building the systems that turn business goals into execution and results.

I have spent most of my career building, growing, fixing, and scaling businesses.

Different companies. Different markets. Different problems.

But one thing has been consistent:

**Whatever the goal is, the business needs a system capable of delivering it.**

That is how I approach management.

Understand the goal.

Understand what is really happening in the business.

Find what is getting in the way.

Decide what matters most.

Build or redesign what is needed.

Get the right people around it.

Execute.

Measure.

Adjust.

Keep moving.

Sometimes the problem is commercial.

Sometimes it is operational.

Sometimes it is leadership.

Sometimes the GTM is wrong.

Sometimes the organization is targeting the wrong customer.

Sometimes there is no clear ownership.

Sometimes the KPIs are measuring activity instead of performance.

Sometimes the opportunity is there, but the organization is simply not built to capture it.

Usually, several of these are connected.

That is why I think in systems.

---

# How I Work

I start with the outcome.

**What are we actually trying to achieve?**

Growth?

Profitability?

A turnaround?

A new market?

A new business?

Better execution?

A stronger commercial engine?

A more scalable organization?

Once the outcome is clear, I work backwards.

**Goal → Reality → Diagnosis → Priorities → System → Execution → Measurement → Adjustment → Result**

That can take me from a P&L review in the morning to a pipeline discussion with sales, a leadership decision, a customer conversation, a partnership negotiation, or an operating problem later that day.

That is part of running a business.

You need to understand the big picture.

You also need to be willing to get into the detail when the detail is where the problem is.

---

# What I Mean by Building Systems

I am not talking about adding process for the sake of process.

A system is simply how the business gets something done consistently.

If the goal is revenue growth, I want to understand:

Who are we targeting?

Why would they buy?

How are we reaching them?

What happens to a lead?

Where are we losing opportunities?

How are we converting?

What are customers worth?

What does it cost to acquire them?

Who owns each part?

What are we measuring?

If the goal is better execution, the questions change:

Are priorities clear?

Does everyone know what they own?

Are the right KPIs in place?

Are decisions being made at the right level?

Are problems visible early?

Do meetings produce decisions?

Are leaders accountable for outcomes?

The system depends on the objective.

# Hands-On Matters

I do not believe senior leadership means staying away from the detail.

The level of involvement should depend on what the business needs.

If the problem is in the pipeline, look at the pipeline.

If conversion is falling, understand why.

If customers are leaving, talk to customers.

If a partnership matters, get involved.

If the team is struggling, understand what is happening.

If the data does not make sense, challenge it.

If a process is slowing the business down, follow it from beginning to end.

Going into the detail does not mean doing the team's job.

It means understanding the business well enough to make better decisions, remove constraints, and help the organization perform.

# Confidentiality

There is another standard I will protect throughout this repository.

**Trust.**

These cases come from real companies and real operating situations.

That does not mean every detail belongs in public.

For that reason:

- Company identities may be generalized in selected cases.
- Specific countries may be described collectively as a region.
- Sensitive internal financial information will not be published.
- Some results may be expressed as percentages, ranges, indices, or directional improvements.
- Customer, employee, partner, and commercially sensitive information will be anonymized.
- Proprietary pricing, contracts, internal processes, and confidential strategy will not be disclosed.

There should be enough evidence to understand the mandate, the decisions, the execution, and the outcomes.

There should never be enough disclosure to compromise the trust of an organization I worked with.
