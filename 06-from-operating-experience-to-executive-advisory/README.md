## 02. Start With the Outcome

A business problem usually appears first as a number or result.

Sales are below target.

Lead generation is weak.

Conversion is falling.

Customer experience is inconsistent.

A market launch is not gaining traction.

Growth has slowed.

Managers are struggling to execute.

The immediate response is often activity.

Generate more leads.

Hire more salespeople.

Increase marketing.

Change the campaign.

Replace a manager.

Buy another technology platform.

Enter another market.

Add AI.

Sometimes those actions are appropriate.

Sometimes they make the problem worse.

The first question should be:

**What outcome are we trying to create?**

Then:

**What system needs to work for that outcome to happen?**

Revenue, for example, can depend on:

**ICP**
↓
**Demand**
↓
**Lead Quality**
↓
**Response**
↓
**Pipeline**
↓
**Conversion**
↓
**Customer Experience**
↓
**Retention**
↓
**Revenue**

If conversion is weak, the answer may not be more sales pressure.

The problem could sit in targeting.

Lead quality.

Qualification.

Response time.

Pricing.

Sales capability.

Customer trust.

Or the process itself.

N34 therefore works backward from the outcome.

The objective is to identify the drivers that matter and build the system around them.

## 03. Diagnose Before Redesigning

Before recommending a new strategy, structure, process, technology, or commercial initiative, I want to understand how the business actually works.

Not how it is supposed to work.

Depending on the mandate, the diagnosis can include:

- Business objective
- Customer and ICP
- Value proposition
- GTM
- Lead generation
- Sales motion
- Pipeline
- Conversion
- Pricing
- Customer experience
- Revenue operations
- Organizational structure
- Roles and responsibilities
- Management capability
- KPIs
- Decision rights
- Operating cadence
- Market-entry assumptions
- Technology
- AI opportunities

The logic is:

**Desired Outcome**
↓
**Current Performance**
↓
**Performance Drivers**
↓
**Operating Reality**
↓
**Constraint**
↓
**Root Cause**
↓
**Required Change**

This prevents a common management mistake:

Trying to improve the wrong part of the business.

More leads will not solve a broken sales process.

A new CRM will not solve weak management discipline.

More KPIs will not solve unclear accountability.

AI will not fix a process that has never been properly designed.

International expansion will not solve weaknesses in the underlying commercial model.

The intervention needs to match the constraint.

> **Understand the system before changing it.**

But advisory work requires more precision.

The organization needs to understand what is changing and why.

The progression became:

**Experience**
↓
**Pattern Recognition**
↓
**Diagnosis**
↓
**System Design**
↓
**Application**
↓
**Measurement**
↓
**Learning**

Experience improves the quality and speed of judgment.

It does not replace diagnosis.

That distinction is central to N34.

> **Experience is most useful when you can separate the principle from the solution and apply the principle to a new problem.**
