# EF Education First | LATAM Business Turnaround & Operating System Redesign

### Rebuilding the operating system behind performance across selected LATAM markets.

Company: EF Education First  
Role: Managing Director  
Geography: LATAM markets  
Business: Multi-product international education business across B2C, B2B, and B2G  
Mandate: Full P&L leadership and regional turnaround  
Focus: Leadership | Operations | GTM | Revenue | Partnerships | Customer Experience | Profitability

---

# Executive Snapshot

I returned to EF to lead a LATAM business that had lost momentum.

This was not one isolated problem.

Revenue was declining.

Lead flow and lead quality had deteriorated.

Conversion was low.

The sales cycle was too long.

Customer acquisition costs were high.

Retention and customer experience needed significant improvement.

Important partnership channels had weakened.

Some parts of the business were underperforming or had lost relevance.

But the biggest issue was deeper.

The organization itself had lost clarity.

EF operated through a complex matrix structure. Multiple functions and stakeholders at HQ were influencing the markets, often with different priorities and different opinions about what needed to happen.

The result was too much direction and not enough ownership.

Communication between HQ and the region was weak.

Decisions were slow.

Local leaders did not always have the authority required to solve local problems.

Sales, marketing, and operations were not working as one system.

Managers lacked clear accountability.

Teams had lost confidence and, in parts of the organization, a clear sense of purpose.

My conclusion was straightforward:

> This was not a sales problem. It was a system problem.

Selling harder would not fix it.

The business needed a reset across leadership, management, GTM, ICP, partnerships, commercial execution, customer experience, KPIs, decision making, and operating discipline.

The goal was not to introduce more process.

The goal was to build a system that made the business easier to understand, easier to manage, and more accountable for results.

# The Business I Inherited

The financial performance was the visible problem.

It was not the root problem.

Several parts of the business had deteriorated at the same time.

## Commercial Performance

The commercial engine was under significant pressure.

At the starting point:

- Lead flow had declined approximately 30%.
- SQL quality had declined approximately 30%.
- Conversion was approximately 1.5%.
- The sales cycle was approximately three months.
- Lead response could take approximately five days.
- Customer acquisition cost was approximately 22%.
- Customer churn was approximately 22%.
- NPS was approximately 35.

These were not independent metrics.

They were signals that the system connecting marketing, sales, customer experience, management, and the market was not working effectively.

---

# The Organizational Problem

The organization was operating inside a complex matrix.

In theory, the matrix should have connected global expertise with local execution.

In practice, it had created confusion.

Different HQ functions could influence the same market.

Different stakeholders had different priorities.

Teams received conflicting direction.

There was no strong communication bridge between HQ and the region.

Decisions moved slowly.

Ownership was unclear.

Local management spent too much time navigating internal complexity and not enough time solving customer and business problems.

This created an important management issue:

> Too many people could influence decisions, but it was not always clear who owned the result.

That affected speed.

It affected accountability.

And eventually, it affected performance.

---

# Leadership and Purpose

The performance problem was also a leadership problem.

Parts of the management layer had become resistant to change.

Accountability was weak.

Morale had deteriorated.

Some strong people had left.

Teams were operating under pressure without enough clarity about where the business was going or what success looked like.

Sales, marketing, and operations were not consistently operating against the same priorities.

The organization needed more than new targets.

It needed clearer leadership.

People needed to understand:

What are we trying to achieve?

Why does it matter?

What do I own?

How will we measure it?

What decisions can I make?

What happens when performance moves off plan?

The turnaround therefore had to address both business performance and organizational confidence.

---

# The GTM Problem

The region did not have enough clarity about where growth should come from.

There was no consistent ICP by segment.

Segmentation was weak.

GTM logic varied.

The commercial funnel lacked discipline.

Sales processes were inconsistent.

CRM data was unreliable.

Forecasting was weak.

Territory ownership was unclear.

Pricing practices were inconsistent.

Resources were not always aligned with the strongest opportunities.

The business was trying to generate growth without a sufficiently clear answer to a basic question:

> Who exactly are we trying to win, through which channel, with what proposition, and who owns the result?

That became one of the first areas we needed to fix.

---

# The Channel Problem

Several important growth channels had weakened.

Relationships across Higher Education had deteriorated.

The K12 pipeline needed to be rebuilt.

The agent vertical was underperforming.

Corporate relationships had lost momentum.

Physical market coverage had also been reduced or underutilized.

This mattered because the business had become too dependent on a narrower set of acquisition routes.

The turnaround therefore needed to do two things at once:

Improve the performance of the existing commercial engine.

And reopen or rebuild additional sources of demand and revenue.

---

# The Management Problem

There was no consistent operating rhythm connecting business performance to management action.

KPIs existed in different places, but they were not operating as one management system.

The organization needed a much clearer connection:

Business goal  
↓  
Business driver  
↓  
KPI  
↓  
Owner  
↓  
Review  
↓

↓

Without that connection, management discussions could become opinion-driven.

And in a matrix organization with many stakeholders, opinion creates even more complexity.

One of my priorities was therefore to make performance more visible and management more evidence-based.

# The Initial Diagnosis

During the first phase of the turnaround, I focused on understanding how the business actually worked.

Not how it was supposed to work.

The diagnosis identified several connected constraints:

## 1. Leadership

- Fragmented leadership
- Weak accountability
- Resistance to change in parts of the management layer
- Poor communication between HQ and the region
- Too many voices influencing local execution
- Slow decision making
- Limited local ownership
- Reduced sense of purpose across parts of the organization

## 2. GTM

- No sufficiently clear ICP by segment
- Weak segmentation
- Inconsistent GTM logic
- Limited localization
- Resources not consistently focused on the highest-value opportunities

## 3. Commercial Engine

- Weak funnel discipline
- Inconsistent sales process
- Long response times
- Long sales cycle
- Low conversion
- Poor CRM hygiene
- Weak forecasting
- Inconsistent pricing
- Unclear territory ownership

## 4. Partnerships and Distribution

- Higher Education relationships had deteriorated
- K12 pipeline needed rebuilding
- Agent channel underperforming
- Corporate HR channel weakened
- Reduced physical market coverage
- Insufficient ownership of strategic partnerships

## 5. Customer Experience

- High churn
- Low NPS
- Weak connection between commercial acquisition and post-sale experience
- Customer feedback was not sufficiently integrated into management decisions

## 6. Operating Model

- No cohesive regional operating system
- Weak connection between strategy and daily execution
- Inconsistent KPIs
- Limited performance visibility
- No sufficiently strong management cadence
- Budget and resources not always aligned with market opportunity

# The Turnaround Blueprint

The turnaround was not built around one initiative.

It was built around one question:

> **What system does the business need to achieve the outcome?**

For this business, I saw six connected layers.

| Layer | What Needed to Work |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Leadership & Ownership** | Clear direction, the right leaders, decision rights, accountability, and stronger local ownership |
| **2. GTM & ICP** | Clear customer priorities, segmentation, positioning, localization, and resource focus |
| **3. Demand & Distribution** | Marketing, Higher Education, K12, agents, corporate relationships, partnerships, and local market coverage |
| **4. Commercial Execution** | Lead management, pipeline, response time, sales process, conversion, pricing, and forecasting |
| **5. Customer Experience & Retention** | Onboarding, service, feedback, NPS, churn, and the connection between acquisition and post-sale performance |
| **6. Revenue & Profitability** | Revenue quality, customer economics, resource allocation, cost discipline, growth, and profitability |

These layers could not be managed independently.

They were connected.

**Leadership & Ownership**  
↓  
**GTM & ICP**  
↓  
**Demand & Distribution**  
↓  
**Commercial Execution**  
↓  
**Customer Experience & Retention**  
↓  
**Revenue & Profitability**

But there was another layer running across all six.

## The Management Layer

**Clear Priorities | KPIs | Owners | Data | Cadence | Decisions | Accountability**

This was what connected the strategy to daily execution.

Without it, the organization could have good people, strong products, market demand, and ambitious targets and still underperform.

---

# How the System Worked

The purpose of the redesign was to create a management loop.

**1. Set the outcome**

What are we trying to achieve?

↓

**2. Identify the drivers**

What actually produces that outcome?

↓

**3. Define the KPIs**

How will we know whether those drivers are working?

↓

**4. Assign ownership**

Who is accountable for each result?

↓

**5. Review performance**

What is happening versus plan?

↓

**6. Diagnose deviations**

Why are we above or below plan?

↓

**7. Make a decision**

What needs to change?

↓

**8. Execute**

Who does what, and by when?

↓

**9. Measure again**

Did the intervention work?

↓

**10. Standardize or correct**

If it works, make it repeatable.

If it does not, understand why and adjust.

Then repeat.

# From Business Goal to Operating Reality

One of the biggest changes was connecting high-level objectives to what people actually did every day.

For example:

| Business Goal | Driver | KPI | Operating Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grow revenue | Qualified demand | SQL volume and quality | Are we generating enough of the right opportunities? |
| Improve conversion | Sales execution | Conversion rate | Where are opportunities being lost and why? |
| Accelerate revenue | Sales velocity | Sales cycle | What is slowing the customer decision? |
| Improve acquisition economics | Marketing and sales efficiency | CAC | Are we spending resources on the right customers and channels? |
| Protect revenue | Customer retention | Churn | Why are customers leaving? |
| Improve customer experience | Delivery and service | NPS | What are customers telling us? |
| Expand distribution | Partnerships | Partner contribution | Which channels are creating productive demand? |
| Restore profitability | Revenue quality and cost discipline | Profitability | Is growth creating economic value? |

The KPI was never the objective.

The KPI was a signal.

The management question was:

> **What is the number telling us about how the system is working?**

# The Operating Cadence

The system needed a rhythm.

Not every problem belongs in the same meeting.

## Weekly

Focus: **Execution**

- Pipeline
- Lead flow
- Lead response
- Conversion
- Sales activity
- CRM quality
- Immediate blockers
- Commitments from the previous week

Question:

> **What needs attention now?**

## Monthly

Focus: **Performance**

- Revenue
- Conversion
- CAC
- Churn
- NPS
- Channel performance
- Partnership contribution
- Team performance
- Budget and resource allocation
- Performance versus plan

Question:

> **Where are we off plan, why, and what are we changing?**

## Quarterly

Focus: **Business Direction**

- Market performance
- Strategic priorities
- Resource allocation
- GTM effectiveness
- Leadership capability
- Business unit performance
- Partnership strategy
- Structural constraints
- What to stop, continue, or scale

Question:

> **Are we still building the right business in the right way?**

# The Principle Behind the Blueprint

The system was designed to reduce the distance between:

**Problem → Visibility → Decision → Action**

The shorter that distance became, the faster the organization could learn and respond.

This was particularly important inside a matrix organization.

The answer to complexity was not more management layers.

It was clearer ownership, better information, stronger communication, and faster decisions.

The objective was simple:

> **Make performance visible. Make ownership clear. Make decisions faster. Keep learning from execution.**

# The Redesign

The intervention was built around several connected moves.

## 1. Reset Leadership and Accountability

I changed parts of the management structure.

Three underperforming mid-level managers were removed.

The management layer was rebuilt.

People were reassigned where stronger commercial or revenue contribution was required.

Responsibilities became clearer.

Local ownership increased.

The objective was not simply to change people.

It was to create a leadership structure capable of making decisions and owning outcomes.

---

## 2. Rebuild GTM Around Clear ICPs

We defined clearer ICPs and segmented the market around priority audiences.

The GTM became more deliberate across areas including:

- K12
- Higher Education
- Young professionals
- Corporate relationships
- Other relevant customer segments

Campaigns became more localized.

Demand generation was reset.

Funnel logic was rebuilt.

More execution authority moved closer to the markets.

The goal was to stop treating LATAM as one generic commercial problem and start focusing resources on the customers and channels with the strongest potential.

---

## 3. Rebuild Partnerships and Distribution

We actively rebuilt channels that had lost momentum.

That included:

- Higher Education
- K12
- Agent networks
- Corporate HR relationships
- Local market coverage

Partnership ownership became clearer.

Coverage increased.

Several physical locations were reopened or reactivated where there was a clear commercial case.

Over time, partnerships contributed approximately 18% to 20% of revenue.

The lesson was important:

Growth did not need to come from one channel.

The system needed multiple productive sources of demand.

---

## 4. Rebuild the Commercial Engine

We redesigned the commercial process around a clearer funnel.

CRM data was cleaned.

Conversion became a management metric.

A 24-hour lead-response standard was introduced.

Sales activity became more disciplined.

Pricing and payment structures were reviewed.

Revenue per customer became more visible.

Territory ownership was clarified.

The sales cycle moved from approximately three months to approximately six weeks.

Conversion increased from approximately 1.5% to approximately 3%.

Customer acquisition cost improved from approximately 22% to approximately 12%.

---

## 5. Rebuild Customer Experience

The turnaround could not stop at acquisition.

Customer experience needed to become part of the revenue system.

Customer surveys were introduced to better understand demand and experience.

Post-sale performance became more visible.

Customer feedback was connected back into management discussions.

NPS increased from approximately 35 to 76.

Churn declined from approximately 22% to approximately 12%.

That mattered commercially.

Better retention protected the revenue we had already acquired.

---

## 6. Install a Real Management System

This was one of the most important changes.

We introduced a consistent operating cadence.

Weekly:

- Manager 1:1s
- Sales reviews
- Pipeline reporting
- CRM and data-quality checks

Monthly:

- Business performance reviews
- KPI review
- Resource and performance intervention

Quarterly:

- Business reviews
- Strategic priorities
- Resource allocation
- Performance against plan

KPIs were implemented across:

- Sales
- Marketing
- Operations
- Customer service

The objective was not more meetings.

The objective was better management.

Every review needed to answer:

What happened?

Why?

Who owns it?

What are we doing about it?

When will we know if it worked?

---

## 7. Use Technology to Improve Execution

Technology had to solve operating problems.

Not create another layer of complexity.

CRM discipline was rebuilt.

Data quality improved.

AI was integrated into Salesforce to support SQL prioritization.

The objective was straightforward:

Help the commercial organization spend more time on the opportunities most likely to matter.

Technology supported the system.

It did not replace management judgment.

---

# What Changed

Within 12 months, the regional operation returned to profitability.

Commercial performance improved materially.

Conversion approximately doubled.

The sales cycle was reduced by approximately 50%.

Lead response moved from approximately five days to 24 hours.

Customer acquisition economics improved significantly.

Churn declined materially.

NPS more than doubled.

Partnership channels were rebuilt and became meaningful contributors to revenue.

Forecasting became more reliable.

CRM data became usable again.

Leadership stabilized.

Teams became more engaged.

Collaboration improved.

Local ownership replaced much of the previous matrix confusion.

And the region regained strategic credibility inside the global organization.

---

# What Actually Drove the Turnaround

There was no single initiative.

That is probably the most important point in this case.

The turnaround came from changing how the parts worked together.

Clearer leadership improved accountability.

Clearer accountability improved execution.

Better ICP definition improved focus.

Better GTM improved demand quality.

Better partnerships expanded distribution.

Better funnel discipline improved conversion.

Better customer experience improved retention.

Better KPIs improved visibility.

Better visibility improved decisions.

Better management cadence increased the speed of intervention.

And better local ownership reduced the friction created by the matrix.

The result was not one better department.

It was a better-performing system.

# The Monday Morning Test

If I had to reduce this turnaround to one practical lesson for another leadership team, it would be this:

> When several KPIs deteriorate at the same time, do not assume you have several independent problems.

Look for the system connecting them.

Revenue decline may be the visible result.

But the cause may sit somewhere else.

Leadership.

Ownership.

ICP.

GTM.

Process.

Customer experience.

Data.

Decision making.

Management cadence.

Or several of them at once.

The executive job is to find those connections, decide what matters, build the system, and stay close enough to execution to know whether it is actually working.
